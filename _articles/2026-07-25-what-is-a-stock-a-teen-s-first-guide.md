**What is a Stock? A Teen's First Guide**
=====================================================

**Difficulty: Beginner**
**Tags: stocks, basics, fundamentals, beginner**

**Introduction**
---------------

Hey there, young investor! Are you curious about the world of stocks and investing? You're in the right place. Understanding what a stock is and how it works can seem daunting, but trust me, it's easier than you think. Imagine owning a tiny piece of your favorite company, like Apple or Nike. That's basically what a stock is. In this article, we'll break down the basics of stocks, why they matter to you, and even give you a chance to try it out yourself.

**What Is It?**
----------------

A stock, also known as equity, is a type of security that represents ownership in a company. When you buy a stock, you're essentially buying a small piece of that company's assets and profits. Think of it like owning a slice of pizza. You're not buying the whole pizza, but you own a part of it.

**Why Should Teens Care?**
---------------------------

So, why should you care about stocks? Well, here's the thing: investing in stocks can be a great way to grow your money over time. Imagine saving up for college or a car, and instead of just letting your money sit in a savings account, you invest it in stocks that could potentially earn you more money. It's like planting a seed and watching it grow into a tree. Of course, there's risk involved, but that's what makes it exciting!

**Key Concepts**
-----------------

Here are the key concepts you need to know about stocks:

* **Shares**: A share is a unit of ownership in a company. When you buy a stock, you're buying a share of that company.
* **Stock market**: The stock market is where stocks are traded (bought and sold). Think of it like a big store where people buy and sell pieces of companies.
* **Portfolio**: A portfolio is a collection of stocks you own. It's like a basket where you keep all your favorite stocks.
* **Dividends**: Dividends are payments made by a company to its shareholders (people who own its stocks). It's like a thank-you note from the company for owning their stock.

**Real-World Examples**
-------------------------

Let's say you buy a share of Apple stock. You now own a tiny piece of Apple, and if Apple makes more money, the value of your share might go up. You could then sell your share for a profit. On the other hand, if Apple has a bad year, the value of your share might go down. That's the risk involved.

Another example is Coca-Cola. Imagine you bought a share of Coca-Cola stock 10 years ago. You would have earned dividends (payments) from the company every quarter, and the value of your share would have likely gone up over time.

3. Research the company's stock price and decide how many shares you can buy with your $100.

**Key Takeaways**
------------------

Here are the main lessons to take away:

* A stock represents ownership in a company.
* Stocks can be bought and sold on the stock market.
* Investing in stocks involves risk, but it can also be a great way to grow your money.
* A portfolio is a collection of stocks you own.
* Dividends are payments made by a company to its shareholders.
