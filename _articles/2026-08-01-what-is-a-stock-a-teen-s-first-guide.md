**What Is It?**
----------------

A stock, also known as equity, represents ownership in a company. Think of it like owning a tiny piece of your favorite company, like Apple or Nike. When you buy a stock, you're essentially buying a small portion of that company's assets and profits.

Imagine you and your friends start a lemonade stand. You each contribute $10 to get started, and you agree to split the profits equally. In this case, each of you owns 1/10th of the lemonade stand, which is similar to owning a stock.

**Why Should Teens Care?**
---------------------------

You might be wondering why you should care about stocks when you're still in school. Here's the thing: understanding stocks can help you make informed decisions about your financial future. By investing in stocks, you can potentially grow your money over time, which can help you achieve your long-term goals, such as:

* Paying for college
* Buying a car or a house
* Traveling the world
* Retiring comfortably

Investing in stocks can also help you develop a sense of financial responsibility and independence.

**Key Concepts**
-----------------

Here are some essential concepts to understand about stocks:

* **Stock price**: The current market value of a stock, which can fluctuate constantly.
* **Shares**: The units of stock that you can buy or sell.
* **Portfolio**: A collection of stocks and other investments that you own.
* **Dividends**: Payments made by companies to their shareholders, usually quarterly or annually.
* **Risk**: The possibility of losing some or all of your investment.

Think of it like this: when you buy a stock, you're essentially betting on the company's future success. If the company does well, the stock price might go up, and you could make a profit. But, if the company struggles, the stock price might drop, and you could lose money.

**Real-World Examples**
-------------------------

Let's look at a few examples of well-known companies and their stocks:

* **Apple (AAPL)**: If you bought Apple stock in 2010, you would have paid around $200 per share. Today, the stock price is over $1,500 per share. That's a significant return on investment!
* **Amazon (AMZN)**: Amazon's stock price has grown from around $50 per share in 2010 to over $3,000 per share today.
* **Nike (NKE)**: Nike's stock price has increased from around $20 per share in 2010 to over $100 per share today.

Keep in mind that these examples are exceptional and not typical. Investing in stocks always involves risk, and there are no guarantees of success.
