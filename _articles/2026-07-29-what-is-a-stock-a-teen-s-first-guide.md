# What is a Stock? A Teen's First Guide
**Difficulty: Beginner**
**Tags: stocks, basics, fundamentals, beginner**

## Introduction
Imagine you own a lemonade stand. You make lemonade, sell it to customers, and earn money. Now, imagine you want to grow your lemonade stand into a huge business. To do that, you need more money to buy more lemons, sugar, and cups. One way to get that money is by asking friends or family to invest in your business. In return, you give them a share of your lemonade stand. This share is like a special ticket that says they own a part of your business.

In the same way, when you buy a stock, you're buying a tiny piece of a big company. This company could be a tech giant like Apple, a retailer like Amazon, or even a movie studio like Disney. In this article, we'll explore what stocks are, why they matter to teens, and how they work.

## What Is It?
A stock, also known as equity, is a type of security that represents ownership in a company. When you buy a stock, you're essentially buying a small piece of that company's assets and profits. Stocks are traded on stock exchanges, like the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) or NASDAQ.

Think of it like a pizza. Imagine a pizza cut into 10 slices. Each slice represents a share of the pizza. If you own one slice, you own 1/10th of the pizza. If the pizza becomes more valuable (e.g., more people want a slice), the value of your slice increases.

## Why Should Teens Care?
You might be thinking, "I'm just a teen, why do I need to know about stocks?" Well, here's the thing: understanding stocks can help you make informed decisions about your financial future. By learning about stocks, you can:

* Start building wealth early
* Make smart investment choices
* Understand the world of business and finance
* Even start your own business one day!

## Key Concepts
Let's break down some key concepts related to stocks:

* **Shares**: A share represents a unit of ownership in a company.
* **Stock price**: The current price of a single share of stock.
* **Market capitalization**: The total value of all outstanding shares of a company.
* **Dividends**: Payments made by a company to its shareholders, usually from profits.
* **Bull market**: A market where stock prices are rising.
* **Bear market**: A market where stock prices are falling.

## Real-World Examples
Let's look at some real-world examples:

* Imagine you bought 10 shares of Apple stock when the price was $100 per share. If the price increases to $150 per share, the value of your shares would increase to $1,500!
* Suppose you invested in Amazon stock when it first went public in 1997. If you held onto those shares, you'd be a millionaire today!
